Interesting book about the great stock market crash in 1929, which brought about the great depression. Was interesting to learn somewhat of a firsthand perspective from the likes of Herbert Hoover FDR Charles Mitchell the CEO of national Citibank, which was the precursor to Citibank the house of Morgan and some other characters. I felt the book was a little bit too much of interesting storytelling and not enough about how the crisis worked and what the causes were of it. It had some of that in it, but it left the reader to kind of discover what they thought instead of The author, Andrew Ross Sorkin, telling the reader what he believes the causes were it had a bit of good information about the founding of the federal reserve through. It’s telling of Carter glasses story, as well as his creation of the Glass-Steagall act you did a good job of painting car glass as both a hero and a complicated person with some terrible qualities. I think it did a good job of painting bankers in general as both victimizers and victims Sorkin seems to believe as I do as well that there were systemic problems, but it wasn’t necessarily the bankers being greedy. It was interesting to learn about Hoover, who I didn’t know much about And the reason that he is considered one of the worst presidents, though there are clearly Structural reasons for why he was in such a bind like being in the gold standard. Interesting to see party politics also play a piece in this as Hoover was a republican and FDR was a Democrat, but also a great liberal in the liberal tradition.

Overall, not exactly what I was looking for but was a page turner and I finished the 430 pages pretty quickly
